## 4. Diff vs the r200 baseline — what round 20 did to the pecking order

Same instrument, same seeds, same game counts; the only differences are round
20's shipped numbers and §2's roster edits. Again: **Elo is zero-sum here**,
so the four big losers below are the *cause* of most of the winners' gains.

### 4.1 The mosquito rework demoted every build that was pairing with it

Round 20.1 replaced the old mosquito (a dmg/kb tax plus an arm/cash trap that
produced **4 on-hit rider procs per armed+cashed pair**) with a clean
**+1 extra ball every [6,5,4] casts**. In steady state that is only
`N/(N−1)` balls per cast = **×1.20 / ×1.25 / ×1.333** — where the old trap
multiplied *rider procs* by ~4 on the pair. Every strategy built on
"mosquito multiplies my per-hit rider" collapsed, and all four moved together:

| strategy | what it paired mosquito with | r200 s1 | now s1 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D3-tycoon | midas (gold per hit) | 1909 (**#1/28**) | 1571 (#12/30) | −338 |
| C6-bolt-combo (Chainer) | frost + gale + lightning | 1504 (#14) | 1251 (#25) | −253 |
| D4-leech | vampire (every-5th-cast heal) | 1701 (#7) | 1455 (#17) | −246 |
| A4-mutations-only | mosquito3 + midas + anger + frost + malady | 1433 (#17) | 1252 (#24) | −181 |

**The baseline's finding #4 ("the champion is economy: midas × mosquito") is
VOID** — that pairing was the old trap's four procs, and it no longer exists.
Midas's own value is untested at the new mosquito; nothing here says midas got
worse on its own. **⚠ The rework is also brand new in human hands**: the
no-push lead ball exists so both balls of a pair can land on a target the
first would otherwise have shoved away — a positioning benefit bots cannot
seek. Treat mosquito's bot number as a floor for now.

### 4.2 Anger rose in RANK while its own knob was nerfed

`markEvery` went `[10,7,5] → [20,15,10]`: an anger lv3 hunter now gets a mark
every 10 s instead of every 5 s — **half the claims, half the ramp**. Yet
anger's two builds moved *up*: **B3-mutation-depth 1850 → 2037 (#2 → #1)** and
**D2-executioner 1670 → 1878 (#9 → #3)**. That is the zero-sum effect: the
four mosquito builds in §4.1 shed ~1000 Elo between them and anger absorbed a
large share of the vacated rank space. The honest statements are:

- Anger is now the **#1 and #3 strategy of 30** in bot hands, at 2037 ≈ a
  **96% pairwise favourite** against an average roster member.
- The nerf did **not** dethrone it in relative terms, and this instrument
  cannot tell you whether it made anger weaker in absolute terms (it has no
  absolute scale). To ask *that*, use an isolation/mirror lab, not this one.
- **D2-executioner closed the gap to B3**: at r200, anger+sustain beat
  anger+chase-mobility by 180 Elo; now the gap is 159. Round 20's cheaper
  boots/ghost is the plausible cause, not anger.
- ⚠ Standing flag (question K): bots claim marks near-perfectly. Anger's
  design cost — *hunting a specific named target while three other people
  shoot you* — is exactly what a bot does not pay.

  plain maxed ember + sustain (D1-warlord 1766). Question M's old worry

## 6. The DPS math: why CDR is not OP

Remi: *"I don't get why CDR isn't OP — double check, results only."* Everything
below is computed from `shared/constants.js` at r207, then checked against a
purpose-built measurement (§6.5). No recommendations.

### 6.1 The constants involved

| thing | value at r207 | source |
|---|---|---|
| fireball (locked lv1 in elemental) | **7 damage**, **2.1 s** cooldown, speed 41, knockback 65 | `SPELLS.fireball` |
| arcane haste (fireball only) | **[18, 32, 32]** — lv3 adds NO haste | `ELEMENTS.arcane.fx.haste` |
| arcane lv3 refund | **1 s off every OTHER cooldown, per enemy hit**, floor 0.25 s, fireball explicitly excluded | `ELEMENTS.arcane.fx.hitRefund` + `arcaneRefund()` |
| hourglass haste (everything) | **[10, 18, 26]** | `ITEM_FX.hourglass.haste` |
| the formula | `cd = base / (1 + haste/100)`, haste **SUMS** across sources | `castSpell()` |
| max stack | arcane 32 + hourglass 26 = **58 haste** → cd 2.100 → **1.329 s = ×1.58 cast rate** | |
| ember | **dmgAdd [1, 2, 4]** — 7 → 8 / 9 / **11** damage | `ELEMENTS.ember.fx` |
| mosquito | pair every **[6,5,4]** balls; trailing balls advance the counter too, and a threshold crossed on a trail is *deferred*, never lost → steady state **N/(N−1)** balls per cast = ×1.20 / ×1.25 / **×1.333** | `mosquitoPair()` |
| costs | arcane 6/5/**12** (23 g maxed) · hourglass 8/8/8 (24 g) · ember 6/5/5 (**16 g**) · mosquito 10/8/8 (26 g) | |

### 6.2 Gold-for-gold, at perfect accuracy

**What these numbers are**: theoretical fireball damage-per-second if *every*
ball lands, computed from the row above. `Δdps` is against the 3.333 dps of a
bare lv1 fireball (7 ÷ 2.1). `Δdps/g` is that gain divided by the gold spent —
the only column that compares shelves fairly.

| purchase | gold | fireball cd | balls/s | dps | Δdps | **Δdps per gold** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — bare fireball — | 0 | 2.100 | 0.476 | 3.333 | — | — |
| arcane1 | 6 | 1.780 | 0.562 | 3.933 | +0.600 | **0.100** |
| arcane2 | 11 | 1.591 | 0.629 | 4.400 | +1.067 | 0.097 |
| arcane3 (no extra haste) | 23 | 1.591 | 0.629 | 4.400 | +1.067 | 0.046 |
| arcane2 + hourglass3 (**max haste 58**) | 35 | 1.329 | 0.752 | 5.267 | +1.933 | 0.055 |
| **arcane3 + hourglass3 (the full CDR core)** | **47** | 1.329 | 0.752 | 5.267 | +1.933 | **0.041** |
| ember1 | 6 | 2.100 | 0.476 | 3.810 | +0.476 | 0.079 |
| ember2 | 11 | 2.100 | 0.476 | 4.286 | +0.952 | 0.087 |
| **ember3** | **16** | 2.100 | 0.476 | 5.238 | +1.905 | **0.119** |
| mosquito3 | 26 | 2.100 | 0.635 | 4.444 | +1.111 | 0.043 |
| ember3 + arcane2 (the warlord core) | 27 | 1.591 | 0.629 | **6.914** | +3.581 | **0.133** |
| E1's core (arcane3 + mosquito3 + hourglass1) | 57 | 1.479 | 0.902 | 6.311 | +2.978 | 0.052 |

**Three things fall out of that table.**

1. **CDR is excellent at level 1 and terrible stacked.** arcane lv1 is
   0.100 dps/g — genuinely competitive with ember lv1's 0.079. The *full*
   47 g CDR core is 0.041 dps/g, **2.9× worse per gold than ember's 16 g**.
2. **The reason is arithmetic, and it was a deliberate round-17 decision.**
   `cd = base/(1+h/100)` is **concave**: the first 18 haste multiplies your
   rate by 1.18; the last 26 (a fully maxed 24 g Hourglass, stacked on
   arcane) multiplies it by only **1.58/1.32 = ×1.197**. Because haste
   **sums** rather than compounds (the round-17 fix that closed question J —
   midas-cdr fell from 86.2% to 24.3%), every further point of haste is worth
   less than the one before it, forever.
3. **The single sharpest comparison**: ember's *last level* costs **5 g** and
   multiplies your damage by 9→11 = **×1.222**. A **fully maxed 24 g
   Hourglass** on top of maxed arcane multiplies your rate by **×1.197**.
   **5 gold of ember beats 24 gold of Hourglass on the same fireball.** That
   is the whole answer to "why isn't CDR OP".

### 6.3 The multiplicative story — what CDR does and does not multiply

CDR's real case is not its own dps; it is that cast rate multiplies **every**
on-hit rider. That case is true for some riders and false for others, and the
distinction is what decides E1 vs E2:

| rider | gated by | does CDR multiply it? |
|---|---|---|
| ember (`dmgAdd`) | per hit | **YES, fully.** ember3 + arcane2 = 6.914 dps, vs 5.238 + 4.400 − 3.333 = 6.305 if the two were merely additive. The **+0.609 cross-term** is real compounding. |
| vampire (`chargeEvery 5` **casts**) | per cast | **YES, fully** — engorged heals per second scale 1:1 with cast rate. |
| midas (gold per hit) | per hit | **YES, fully.** |
| frost (`stacksToTrigger 3`) | per hit | **YES** — triggers per second scale with hit rate. |
| mosquito (pair every N **balls**) | per ball | **YES** — pairs/s scale with ball rate, but the pair is *itself* only ×1.333 balls, so this compounds a small number. |
| malady (2 hits to infect, then `once-per-instance immunity`) | per **target**, not per hit | **BARELY.** The second hit lands sooner; after that the target is immune to that instance. Faster casting buys you the ramp-up, not more infections. |
| **anger** (mark every `[20,15,10]` s, claim = +0.5 dmg forever) | **the clock** | **NO.** At lv3 a mark appears every 10 s. A base-cadence seat gets ~4.8 casts inside that window; a max-CDR seat gets ~7.5. At the ~60% per-ball hit rate measured in §6.5 that is P(claim) 98.7% vs 99.9% — **CDR buys anger about +1%.** The roster's #1 strategy is the one CDR cannot help. |
| **the fireball's own cooldown** | — | **NO, by design.** `arcaneRefund()` explicitly skips `fireball`; the self-refund was measured as a 74% feedback loop and the guard is a scar, not a knob. |

**arcane lv3's refund, priced honestly.** It gives 1 s per enemy hit off every
*other* cooldown. A refund rate of R seconds-per-second is arithmetically the
same as **+100·R Ability Haste on the whole kit**. Measured fireball hit rates
in §6.5 are 0.25 hits/battle-second (no CDR) to 0.37 (max CDR), so the refund
is worth roughly **+25 to +37 haste on everything except the fireball** — i.e.
comparable to a second maxed Hourglass, for 12 g. That is genuinely strong,
**and it is why E2 (five cooldowns to shave) beat D7 (one) by 166 Elo and E1
(two, plus a mosquito) by 259.** It is also why the refund is not a runaway:
it cannot touch the fireball, so it never accelerates its own source.

### 6.4 Does the bot actually cast on cooldown?

**Yes — 99.6% of the theoretical cadence.** The Hard berserker's decision
interval is `react: [0.16, 0.10]` = 0.16-0.26 s, mean 0.21 s, and its fireball
branch fires whenever `cooldowns.fireball <= 0`. Half a tick of granularity
(~0.105 s) costs ~5.0% of a 2.100 s cooldown but ~7.9% of a 1.329 s one, which
predicts a realized ×1.538 instead of the nominal ×1.580. **Measured: ×1.575**
(§6.5). So the instrument is *not* under-reading CDR through lazy bots — this
is very nearly the best case CDR can have.

### 6.5 Measured: more balls ≠ proportionally more damage

**What this is**: a purpose-built probe (scratch tool, not committed) run
because "more balls" is only worth something if the balls land. Each game is a
4-seat Hard-bot elemental lobby: **2 control seats buying an items-only list
(sword/amulet/boots/treads/cape ×3) and 2 probe seats buying the probe FIRST
and then the identical items list**. Seats rotate every game. 500 games × 2
seeds per probe; the two seeds agreed to within 0.5% on every cell, so one
averaged figure is shown. `dmg` is `pl.dmgDealt` (lava excluded, **overkill
excluded** — so it is a lower bound). `balls` counts every fireball leaving
the muzzle, mosquito trailing balls included.

| probe (bought first, then the same item list) | gold | balls/s vs control | **dmg per ball vs control** | dmg/game vs control | dmg/game gain **per gold** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ember3 (7 → 11 damage) | 16 g | ×1.09 (see note) | **×1.451** | **×1.583** | **+3.6% / g** |
| mosquito3 (pair every 4th ball) | 26 g | ×1.342 | **×0.927** | ×1.244 | +0.94% / g |
| arcane3 + hourglass3 (58 haste) | 47 g | ×1.575 | **×0.946** | ×1.490 | +1.04% / g |

*Note on ember's ×1.09 balls/s*: ember changes no cooldown. The rate column is
normalised by total battle time, and an ember seat survives more of that time
(6.4 → 13.8 kills/game), so it gets to fire during more of it. Ignore that
cell; ember's story is entirely the dmg-per-ball column.

Read the middle column: **every extra ball is worth 5-7% less than the balls
that came before it.** Both throughput axes pay that tax; the damage axis does
not (ember's ×1.451 is below its theoretical ×1.571 only because overkill is
excluded from `dmgDealt` — a bigger ball wastes more of its damage on a dying
target). Per gold, **ember converts ~3.5× better than either throughput axis**,
which is the §6.2 theory reproduced in a live game.

**Why the tax exists — the knockback economy.** A fireball carries 65
knockback. Firing faster means your target spends more of its time being
shoved: out of your ~8.5-unit prowl ring, and out of the *next* ball's path.
This is exactly the scar behind the mosquito rework ("*constant knockback
throws the victim out of a follow-up ball's path: a multi-ball effect wants
one of its balls to NOT push*"), and the pair's zero-push lead is the design
answer to it. Note that mosquito still pays a slightly *larger* per-ball tax
(−7.3%) than raw CDR (−5.4%) — the trailing ball flies 0.15 s behind on the
**same aim**, so its hit or miss is highly correlated with the lead's.

⚠ **What this probe is not**: it compares each axis against *items*, and §4.3
says items are the weakest shelf in the game. The kills columns (control
6.4-10.7 vs probe 13.8-14.0 per game) therefore say "this beats buying items",
not "this is strong in absolute terms". The trustworthy columns are the
*ratios* — balls/s and dmg-per-ball — which are measured inside the same
lobby, on the same opponents, in the same games.

### 6.6 The three-line answer

1. **Haste sums and `cd = base/(1+h/100)` is concave**, so stacked CDR decays:
   the full 47 g CDR core buys 0.041 dps/gold against ember's 0.119 — 5 g of
   ember lv3 (×1.222 damage) beats a maxed 24 g Hourglass (×1.197 rate).
2. **CDR multiplies most riders but not the two that matter most right now**:
   never the fireball's own cooldown (the anti-feedback-loop guard), and
   effectively never anger (+1%), which is the #1 strategy on the table.
3. **Extra balls convert at a 5-7% discount** (measured), because knockback
   pushes the target out of the next ball's path — so the throughput axes lose
   again to the damage axis, which pays no such tax. Where CDR *does* shine is
   arcane lv3's refund (≈ +25-37 haste on the rest of your kit for 12 g), and
   that is a **kit-width** effect, not a fireball effect — E2 1697 vs E1 1438.

## 7. What this instrument cannot see (state it beside every number above)

- **Bots don't lead targets beyond a simple intercept, bait, pre-cast, or
  chain CC windows.** Reactive tools (shield, blink, boomerang recall-catch)
  and cluster effects (malady's contagion, gale's gust positioning) read at a
  FALSE floor: D5-plaguebearer and D6-sumo are bot artifacts, not verdicts.
- **The boomerang is a known over-read** (nothing in the roster dodges or
  catches one) — C4's #2 finish is inflated for the same reason it was at
  r200.
- **Anger saturates bot instruments**: bots claim marks near-perfectly, so
  none of these tables can price anger's *design cost* (hunting a named target
  under fire). Question K stands.
- **Mosquito's rework has never been in human hands.** The no-push lead exists
  to let both balls of a pair land on a target that would otherwise have been
  shoved away — a positioning payoff a bot never seeks. §4.1's collapse is a
  bot floor.
- **Elo here is a ranking, not a strength meter** (§1). It cannot answer "did
  the anger nerf make anger weaker" — only "did anger move relative to the
  other 29 strategies".
- **Five item rows under-spend their budget by 25-60 g** (§2) and spill into
  the exhaust tail's elements; their reads are diluted upward.
- **One tail-behaviour change since r200**: commit `d486795` let order-driven
  builds unlock the in-game random shopping fallback. The ELO lists append the
  shared exhaust pass three times, which already covers every element, so this
  should bite only in the rare seat that maxes everything — but it is a
  difference between the two tables, and it is stated here rather than assumed
  harmless.
